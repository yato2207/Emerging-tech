Enterprise LLM Adoption and B2B Trust in Cybersecurity

“For a manager working with emerging technology, the scarce resource is not capability. It is warranted trust, and it is built by whoever makes verification cheap.”

This briefing synthesizes a two-case qualitative study conducted at the Department of Management Studies, IIT Delhi, based on three practitioner interviews: the CTO of an AI-native firm (Case 1, LLM adoption) and a conceptual buyer-vendor dyad of Client C1 and Vendor V1 in enterprise cybersecurity (Case 2).

Regulation & Theoretical Framing (Case 1)

CTO's Reading of RBI Liability: Said directly · 19:12

“The bank is liable.” Banks are “very scared” because legal responsibility sits with the bank—not the model vendor—if PII leaks or an LLM errs.

Counterpoint — RBI FREE-AI Report (2025): Inferred

The RBI FREE-AI framework proposes graded liability and focuses on enabling AI while safeguarding against risk—turning fear into Indian data residency, PII stripping, and human sign-off.

TOE Lens: Technology (hallucination limits), Organisation ($6–10M cost & token budgets), Environment (RBI liability & “Instagram type adoption” hype at 32:05).

TAM Lens: Perceived usefulness determined every adoption choice; perceived ease of use never surfaced for skilled engineers.

Education beats reassurance: C1 values risk diagnostics on real gaps over “we are secure” claims: “I would consider education more valuable than reassurance.” (24:24, EDUCATE)

The Three-Legged Stool & Visibility Paradox

1. Contracts (Floor): SLAs, breach-notice timelines, audit rights, data residency, liability. “I actually don't believe in contractual agreements alone.” (C1, 8:00)

2. Relationship & Value: Candour and conservative honesty. “Neither is sufficient... we'll have to do both.” (V1, 4:57)

3. Client's Own Verification: Dashboards, DLP, DRM, dark-web checks. “The risks also keep increasing. So is our task.” (C1, 14:05)

Visibility Paradox: C1 wants full insight, while V1 shares certs/audits/open defects yet keeps deep architecture verbal because written blueprints “would become a risk for us as well.”

C1's Incident Response by Root Cause (Table 2.9)
Vendor hid or delayed the news Blacklist the vendor
Vendor came forward but was careless End contract, no blacklist
New, hard-to-stop attack (e.g., WannaCry) Stay; “we definitely give them a cover”
First breach, handled openly and well “A second chance”
“The vendor's response is more important than the incident itself.” (C1, 16:57)

Switching Costs & Asset Specificity (TCE)

“Switching comes at a cost. It's never free.” (C1, 32:59)

Data migration: Moving logs, labels, and repositories safely.
Integration: Re-wiring connectors and identity workflows.
Retraining: Re-onboarding admins and staff.
SIEM / SOC rebuild: Rebuilding detection rules and dashboards.

Commitment-Trust & TCE: High switching costs breed pre-sale caution—yet repeated breaches force exit “even if it comes at a heavier cost.” Offering exit-assistance clauses up front lowers ex-ante fear.

03 · Strategic Synthesis (Company A → Company B)

Company A's Trust Playbook & Dyadic Alignment

Five trust-building roles mapped across four lifecycle stages to resolve Company B's three worries: (a) safety vs. third-party data control, (b) adoption inertia, and (c) privacy & trust.

01 · PRE-SALE

Educator & Evidence Provider

Run a free risk check on client data to expose real leaks, and deliver a standard Trust Pack (ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, audits, POC).

02 · CONTRACTING

Co-Governor

Proactively codify SLAs, breach-notice timelines, audit rights, data residency, and exit-assistance clauses.

03 · ONBOARDING

Enabler

Deliver role-based training (“enforce, enable, govern”) so users do not downgrade sensitivity labels to get work done (V1, 31:32).

04 · RETENTION

Transparent Communicator

Maintain roadmap candour (“what we will and will not build”) and run a joint incident drill against an agreed breach playbook.

Sector: Government

Lead with formal compliance standards, auditability, and deployment sovereignty.

Sector: Banks / BFSI

Lead with customer PII protection, Indian data residency, and RBI FREE-AI / GDPR safeguards.

Sector: Manufacturing

Lead with intellectual property protection, trade secrets, and partner-perimeter controls.
